Smart · App Map

What powers what

Smart tracks trials and subscriptions by the card they charge, and emails a reminder before you're billed, at the time you choose, with the exact place to cancel.

The main pieces

  • The appScreens in your browser. templates/index.html, static/js/
  • The serverSaves data, answers the app. app.py
  • The databaseOne file, smart.db. Only db.py touches it.
  • The watcherDecides when to remind. reminders.py
  • The mailerWrites and sends the email. mailer.py + Gmail

One action, end to end: saving something that's due → the email

The route through the code

Followed during the wrap-up. Line numbers can drift as code changes; the names in code are stable things to search for.

  1. You tap Save. The form gathers what you typed and posts it to the server.
    static/js/screens/forms.js → search api.saveSubscription(values); the request itself is saveSubscription in static/js/api.js
  2. The server saves it, then asks whether a reminder is due. Saving and reminding are kept separate on purpose.
    app.py → post_subscription, the line reminders.check_one(...)
  3. Deciding when. next_reminder finds the next moment (2 days before, 1 day before, or "today", at your chosen time, in your time zone). If it has passed, the mailer is called; only if Gmail accepts is it recorded, so failures retry and successes never repeat.
    reminders.py → next_reminder, _check (which calls db.mark_reminded in db.py); the every-minute loop is start_watcher
  4. Sending. Fill in the email templates, open an encrypted connection to Gmail, log in with the app password, hand over the message.
    mailer.py → build_reminder (the subject line), send (SMTP_SSL, login, send_message)
    with smtplib.SMTP_SSL(SMTP_HOST, SMTP_PORT, timeout=20) as server:
    server.login(address, app_password.replace(" ", ""))
    server.send_message(msg)

A practice to reuse

Prove the riskiest unknown with a tiny test of its own, before anything depends on it, and when something breaks, test one layer at a time.

In this build, the first step was send_test_email.py: one real email through Gmail before the app existed. Later, when reminders stopped arriving, a separate connection test showed Gmail's port 587 timing out while port 465 connected instantly. That proved it was the network, not the code, and the fix was one line in mailer.py (recorded under Revisions in devpost/checklist.md). Another missing email turned out to be a server still running old code plus a reminder address nobody could see in the app, which is why Smart now shows "Reminders go to …".

On your next project, ask early: which part could sink the whole idea? Write the smallest script that proves it works, and keep it around for when it doesn't.
